01

Introduction

Tell me about yourself

I currently work in Customer Service at StubHub/viagogo. I started in Seller Support and progressed to Hybrid, supporting buyers, fan sellers and brokers across chat, email and phone. I handle complex cases, apply policy and escalate potential risks.

Before StubHub, I worked in financial planning at AIA, including compliance and fraud-related checks. I also worked in accounting, reviewing records and investigating discrepancies. My degree is in Business Analytics.

Buyer Fraud brings together the areas I enjoy: understanding customer behaviour, checking transaction information and making careful decisions. I would bring frontline marketplace knowledge and a willingness to learn the team's specialist tools.

  • Current role: Seller Support → Hybrid; buyers and sellers.
  • Background: financial risk, accounting and analytics.
  • Goal: investigate buyer risk and make evidence-based decisions.
02

Why Buyer Fraud?

Why this role, and why now?

Working across Seller Support and Hybrid has given me a broader understanding of the marketplace. I am especially interested in cases where the details do not fit and need a closer look. I want to build on that experience by reviewing higher-risk transactions, checking account history and helping protect genuine customers.

Why should we choose you?

I understand the marketplace from both buyer and seller support. I am used to applying policy, handling sensitive information and explaining difficult decisions calmly. My financial and analytics background also helps me check evidence carefully. I would need training on the team's fraud tools, but I would bring useful frontline experience from day one.

  • Marketplace knowledge + careful checks + clear communication.
  • Learn the team's tools, policies and decision limits.

Crypto experience

Tell me about your mining project

I started cryptocurrency mining during university and ran an independent project. I used mining pools across multiple platforms to spread my exposure, and checked payments by reviewing wallet-address activity.

Later, I took part in forum-based fiat-to-crypto and crypto-to-fiat markets. I reviewed seller profiles and behavioural patterns, and checked possible wallet connections to services such as Tornado Cash.

That experience taught me to consider transaction records and counterparty behaviour together. In Buyer Fraud, I would apply the same habit of checking the evidence and considering legitimate explanations, using the team's approved process.

Does a mixer connection prove fraud?

No. A possible connection needs context and supporting evidence. I would distinguish a direct interaction from an indirect link and be clear about what I could not establish.

Fraud case practice

Hypothetical cases. Routing reflects the supplied 22 July 2026 guide; check current procedures during training.

“I did not authorise this charge.”

I would acknowledge the concern and follow the approved identity checks. I would establish whether this is a buyer charge, an authorisation hold, a seller fulfilment charge or an existing chargeback.

For a qualifying unauthorised buyer charge, I would collect the required details through approved channels, document the customer's report and escalate to Buyer Fraud. I would explain the next step and advise them to contact their payment provider to secure the payment method.

  • Classify the issue before routing.
  • No full card details or promised refunds.

High-value order with inconsistent information

I would check the event deadline, account history, transaction details, recent changes and prior notes. I would look for supporting evidence and consider explanations such as a gift purchase or travel.

I would follow approved verification and decision criteria. If the uncertainty exceeded my authority, I would escalate with the evidence, missing information and urgency clearly documented.

  • One unusual detail is a reason to check, not a verdict.
  • Balance potential loss with the impact on a genuine buyer.

Tickets sent to an unknown email; event in six hours

I would check whether this is a delivery issue or confirmed account takeover. I would review the notes for Fraud Team confirmation and any account block. Re-transfer complaints follow the delivery workflow; confirmed takeover follows the account-protection and compensation routes.

An unresolved qualifying Buyer Fraud case with an event within 24 hours meets the supplied guide's supervisor urgency criterion. I would flag the event time and evidence without promising an unblock or refund.

  • Unknown email alone does not confirm account takeover.
  • Check the case type, confirmation and urgency.

Routing & communication

Routing reminders

  • Chargeback: Chargebacks route.
  • Payment review or bank hold: payment-state guidance.
  • Accidental or acknowledged family purchase: applicable sales/relisting policy.
  • Direct buyer-seller contact: check first occurrence, repeat contact and available proof.
  • Phishing or impersonation: preserve evidence and use the approved safety route.
  • Unclear procedure: check the current article or ask a supervisor; avoid unsupported timelines.

Handoff script

The customer reports [claim]. Records show [facts and timestamps]. I checked [sources] and completed [actions]. The unresolved concern is [discrepancy]. The event is at [date/time]. I am requesting [next action]. Supporting details are in the approved fields.

Speaking to a worried customer

I understand why this is concerning, especially with the event approaching. I have documented the issue and need to check it through our approved process. I will explain the next step, but I cannot confirm the outcome before the review is complete.

Potential phishing message

Please avoid the links in that message and use the official app or website to contact support. We can check the concern through the approved channel.

Quick answers

How would you prioritise a busy queue?

I would follow the team's priority rules, considering potential harm, event deadlines, case age and what action is still possible. I would flag urgent cases for supervisor attention and keep ownership and next steps clear.

What if you disagree with another agent?

I would compare the evidence and policy, explain my concern and listen for anything I missed. If the decision remained unclear or exceeded my authority, I would involve the appropriate reviewer.

Tell me about a mistake

I initially [real mistake]. I noticed it when [signal]. I corrected it by [action] and informed [relevant person]. Since then, I have used [specific check] to prevent it happening again.

How would you share a new fraud pattern?

I would check that the cases show a repeated pattern, summarise the common features and note any uncertainty or legitimate explanation. I would share relevant case references through the approved team channel.

Your first 30, 60 and 90 days?

First, I would learn the tools, policies and decision limits. Next, I would focus on handling cases consistently and applying feedback. As I become more confident, I would document recurring issues and share useful observations with the team.

Final review

Five points to remember

  • I know both sides of the marketplace.
  • I check customer explanations against records.
  • I bring financial risk awareness and analytical skills.
  • I separate suspicion from evidence.
  • I communicate clearly and work within my authority.

Questions to ask: choose two or three

  • What does success look like in the first three months?
  • How do you balance decision quality, speed and customer impact?
  • How are new agents trained on difficult cases?
  • Where could my Seller Support and Hybrid experience help most?

Closing script

This role combines my marketplace knowledge, customer experience and interest in investigation. I would bring careful evidence review, clear communication and a willingness to learn. I am excited about the opportunity to help protect customers and the business.
