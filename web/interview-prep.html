Looking back, how do you feel about your decisions?

My decisions may include false positives or missed risks. Unusual behaviour deserves checking, not an automatic fraud label. I would use feedback to understand which evidence I over- or underweighted.

How would you acknowledge possible false positives?

I may have flagged legitimate behaviour. I would test explanations such as travel, gifts or shared households against evidence, then use approved verification or escalation where uncertainty remains.

Would you change an answer now?

Only if meaningful new evidence or an overlooked fact changed the reasoning. I would explain what I knew, what remained uncertain and which fact would change my conclusion.

How would you evaluate your performance?

Without confirmed outcomes, I cannot claim accuracy. I would review false positives, missed fraud, reasoning quality and time management, then look for repeated mistakes.

What will you bring into the interview?

One real decision: strongest signal, legitimate explanation, unresolved gap and one improvement. Be clear where your support role ended and the fraud reviewer took over.

06

Fraud case practice

Hypothetical cases. Answer: facts → risks and positives → decisive gap → action within your authority. Verify current internal procedures.

“I did not authorise this charge.”

Acknowledge the concern and use approved identity checks. Distinguish an unauthorised buyer charge from a hold, seller charge or existing dispute. Document and route appropriately; do not request full card details or promise a refund.

High-value order with inconsistent information

Check account history, recent changes, payment details and the event deadline. Test legitimate explanations such as travel or gifts. Use approved verification; escalate unresolved risk with the evidence and missing information.

Tickets sent to an unknown email; event in six hours

Separate delivery trouble from possible account takeover; an unknown email is not confirmation. Check notes and account status, use the correct route and flag urgency without promising an unblock or refund.

Payment succeeded and tickets were delivered. Does that resolve an unauthorised-charge concern?

Payment and delivery do not necessarily establish authorisation. Check the reported concern and supporting records through the approved process.

One person calls it a family gift; another denies authorising the charge. What next?

Record both accounts. Use approved identity and authorisation checks, preserve contradictions and escalate unresolved evidence. Do not choose the more persuasive speaker.

Several small purchases precede a much larger order. What would you check?

Examine timing, payment attempts, account history and related activity. Consider ordinary purchases as well as possible testing; the sequence alone proves neither.

The event starts in 15 minutes and your review is incomplete. What do you do?

Identify the decisive gap and alert the authorised reviewer immediately. Document available options and communicate uncertainty without promising clearance or skipping required checks.

The customer’s timeline conflicts with transaction timestamps. What next?

Check timezone, timestamp meaning and source reliability first. Ask a focused clarification, then document any remaining inconsistency.

An important field is missing. Would you assume the check passed?

Treat missing information as unknown. Seek an approved alternative source, explain the decision impact and follow the permitted route if uncertainty remains.

Purchase volume spikes during a popular presale. How would you assess it?

Confirm the sale window and compare similar buyers and events. Investigate unexplained differences instead of treating the whole traffic spike as suspicious.

Routing & communication

How would you choose the correct route?

Distinguish fraud concerns, chargebacks, payment holds, delivery issues and acknowledged purchases. Check the current article and decision limits. If unclear, ask the authorised reviewer rather than invent a timeline or outcome.

Give a concise handoff

Customer reports [claim]. Records show [facts/timestamps]. I checked [sources] and took [actions]. The gap is [uncertainty]. Event: [date/time/timezone]. Requested next action: [request].

What would you say to a worried customer?

“I understand the concern, especially with the event approaching. I have documented the issue and will follow our review process. I can explain the next step, but cannot confirm the outcome yet.”

How would you handle a potential phishing message?

Ask the customer to avoid the message’s links and contact support through the official app or website. Preserve relevant evidence through approved channels and follow the safety process.

A buyer asks exactly what triggered the review. What would you say?

Explain the review and permitted next steps plainly. Share authorised information without exposing detection details or accusing the buyer.

A buyer refuses additional verification. How would you respond?

Explain the purpose and approved options, acknowledge concerns and document the refusal. Follow the process; refusal alone is not proof of fraud.

A frustrated buyer demands an immediate guarantee. What would you say?

Acknowledge the impact, state confirmed facts and explain the next action. Give only supported timing and avoid guarantees outside your authority.

Your shift ends with an urgent case unresolved. How do you hand it over?

Confirm ownership. Summarise evidence, completed checks, the event deadline, pending action and the customer update needed through the approved handover process.

Algorithms & metrics

Interview ideas, not claims about the company’s systems or tools you have deployed. Learn the purpose, one example and one limitation.

How could analytics support Buyer Fraud?

Start with understandable velocity rules and a review queue. Models can combine signals; graphs connect cases; behaviour changes suggest further checks. Measure losses and genuine-buyer friction together. These are approaches I want to learn.

What is a sliding-window velocity rule?

Count recent attempts or payment failures over a time window. A burst can trigger review, but ticket releases, outages or shared households may explain it. Check supporting evidence.

How would a risk model help?

Logistic regression or boosted trees can combine signals such as account changes and payment failures. A score guides investigation; it is not proof. Check calibration, reviewed outcomes and legitimate explanations.

Where would graph analysis help?

Connect accounts, devices and tokenised payment identifiers to find related cases. Verify each link and timeline: shared networks or household devices can create innocent connections.

How could you spot account takeover?

Review the sequence: new-device access, contact changes and unusual purchases. Compare with account history and approved identity checks; a replacement phone or travel can explain individual changes.

What is anomaly detection useful for?

Methods such as Isolation Forest surface unfamiliar combinations for investigation. Compare similar buyer/event groups. Unusual behaviour is a lead, not a reason for automatic rejection.

How would you prioritise risk before an event?

Combine estimated preventable harm, deadline, case age and review effort. Urgency changes the review priority, not whether the buyer is fraudulent; consider customer friction and available authorised actions.

How would you know a fraud model works?

Use reviewed outcomes and later-period testing with only decision-time information. Track precision, recall, fraud loss, false declines and review time. Check differences across buyer/event groups and monitor changing patterns.

Which approach would you suggest first?

Understand the existing workflow, then test a simple, explainable baseline. Add complexity only if it improves outcomes within review capacity. My contribution is clear case evidence and feedback from frontline experience.

Two analysts have different fraud detection rates. Is the higher rate better?

Compare case mix, denominators, confirmed outcomes and false positives. A higher rate alone does not establish better judgment or customer protection.

What if a high risk score conflicts with reassuring case evidence?

Check the score’s available reasons, source quality and contrary evidence. Follow approved decision rules and document why the conflict needs further review.
